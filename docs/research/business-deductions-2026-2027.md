**Checked:** September 15, 2026. **Jurisdiction:** U.S. federal income tax. **Purpose:** a bounded source foundation for WriteOff’s “Can I write this off?” assistant, particularly business vehicles. This is not a complete tax-code corpus or a determination of any user’s return.

## 2. Business-expense decision rules

1. Identify the taxpayer, business activity, tax year, and specific business purpose. §162 covers ordinary and necessary costs of carrying on a trade or business. Common and accepted costs can be ordinary; helpful and appropriate costs can be necessary. An LLC, business credit card, or merchant category does not establish this connection. [§162(a)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title26-section162&num=0&edition=prelim), [Publication 334, chapter 8](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p334).
2. Separate personal and business portions. Personal living costs do not become business expenses because they help someone work. A mixed-use phone/internet bill needs a supported allocation; a personal grocery receipt does not establish a business meal. Basic local service for the first residential telephone line has a specific personal-expense rule. [§262](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title26-section262&num=0&edition=prelim), [IRS Income & Expenses FAQ](https://www.irs.gov/faqs/small-business-self-employed-other-business/income-expenses/income-expenses-1).
3. Determine current expense versus an asset or other special category before assigning an immediate deduction. Equipment, vehicles, improvements, inventory, and startup costs can require different treatment. A bank charge alone cannot determine timing or basis. Route unresolved capitalization/accounting issues for further research. [Publication 334](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p334), [IRS business-expense resources](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/guide-to-business-expense-resources).
4. Apply the relevant restriction. Ordinary client meals generally face the 50% limit, business-purpose and nonlavish requirements, and taxpayer/employee presence; exceptions need their own authority. Entertainment generally is nondeductible, and business discussion does not make tickets deductible. Gifts generally have a $25 annual per-recipient limit, subject to specific exclusions. Do not reuse the temporary restaurant-meal rule that ended after 2022. [§274(a), (b), (k), (n)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title26-section274&num=0&edition=prelim).
5. Match substantiation to the category. Keep amount, date, item/service, payee, proof of payment, business purpose, and relevant business relationship. Travel, gifts, and listed property have heightened §274(d) requirements. A receipt helps establish a purchase; it does not by itself establish business purpose, allocation, ownership, or tax eligibility. [§274(d)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title26-section274&num=0&edition=prelim), [IRS recordkeeping](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/recordkeeping).

Employee questions need an early branch. Ordinary unreimbursed W-2 employee expenses are not automatically Schedule C deductions; the OBBBA made the miscellaneous-itemized-deduction disallowance permanent, with particular statutory exceptions. Route eligible educators, reservists, fee-basis officials, qualified performing artists, reimbursements, and employer-provided vehicles to their applicable rules. [Notice 2026-10 §3](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-26-10.pdf).

## 3. Vehicle eligibility questions

Ask only unanswered questions that affect the next conclusion:

- Which business uses the vehicle, who owns or leases it, and is the user self-employed or an employee?
- What are the acquisition/contract date and the date it became ready and available for its intended business use? Purchase, payment, and placed-in-service dates are distinct. [Publication 946, Placed in Service](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p946).
- What are cost/basis, new/used status, seller relationship, trade-in or other basis adjustments, and any earlier personal use?
- What are make, model, model year, exact configuration, manufacturer vehicle class, and the actual vehicle’s documented weight rating? For trucks/vans, verify GVWR; for other passenger automobiles, do not substitute GVWR for the statutory unloaded-weight measure. [§280F(d)(5)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title26-section280F&num=0&edition=prelim).
- If claiming an SUV-cap exclusion: how many seats are behind the driver, what is the cargo area’s **interior** length and accessibility, or does the vehicle meet the exact enclosure/body configuration?
- What are total, business, commuting, and other personal miles for the year, supported by records? A projected percentage is provisional, and company logos do not convert personal trips into business trips. [Publication 946, How To Allocate Use](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p946).
- Was standard mileage, §179, bonus, or accelerated depreciation used before? Are five or more vehicles operated simultaneously? Is this a lease?
- For §179: what other qualifying property was placed in service, what active-business taxable income is available, and what carryovers/elections apply?
- Will business use fall to 50% or less, or will the vehicle be sold, transferred, or converted to personal use?

### Weight and photos

For most vehicles IRS guidance says GVWR can be found on the driver door post. Request a legible certification-label image or exact-vehicle manufacturer documentation, then confirm the recorded value and units. An exterior photo or guessed model/trim **cannot establish GVWR**. A label photo may support weight; it cannot establish ownership, acquisition date, business purpose, mileage percentage, or deduction eligibility. This evidence-handling rule is a product inference from the classification and substantiation requirements. [Publication 225, Listed Property](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p225), [§274(d)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title26-section274&num=0&edition=prelim).

### Classification and depreciation sequence

**A. Apply the correct vehicle definition.** A road vehicle with four wheels may be a §280F passenger automobile if within the statutory 6,000-pound threshold. Trucks/vans use gross vehicle weight; other automobiles use unloaded gross vehicle weight. Exactly 6,000 pounds is not “over 6,000.” Exceeding the applicable threshold can remove passenger-auto dollar caps while leaving the vehicle as listed transportation property. [§280F(d)(4)–(5)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title26-section280F&num=0&edition=prelim), [Publication 946, Other Property Used for Transportation](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p946).

**B. Test the heavy-SUV cap separately.** The §179 heavy-SUV category generally covers passenger-carrying road vehicles above the passenger-auto threshold and at or below 14,000 pounds GVWR. Exclusions include: seating for more than nine people behind the driver; qualifying cargo space at least six feet in interior length with the statutory open/capped and passenger-access conditions; or the specified fully enclosed vehicle with no seating behind the driver and limited front protrusion. A pickup badge alone does not prove an exclusion. Above 14,000 pounds is outside this SUV cap, not automatic permission for a full deduction. [§179(b)(5)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title26-section179&num=0&edition=prelim).

**C. Test qualified business use.** Listed vehicles generally require **more than 50%** qualified business use for §179, bonus, and accelerated depreciation. Exactly 50% fails. At 50% or less, business operating expenses may still qualify; depreciation follows the alternative/straight-line rules. Investments and certain related-owner compensation uses do not count as qualified business use. Specialized nonpersonal vehicles have distinct exceptions; do not apply the ordinary-SUV branch mechanically. [§280F(b), (d)(6)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title26-section280F&num=0&edition=prelim), [§168(k)(2)(D)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title26-section168&num=0&edition=prelim).

**D. Compute eligible business basis, then apply elections/limits.** §179 requires qualifying purchased property for active business use and has aggregate, phaseout, business-income, and sometimes SUV limits. Related-party/carryover-basis acquisitions can fail. Apply any elected §179 before bonus to the remaining eligible basis; avoid deducting the same basis twice. [§179(b), (d)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title26-section179&num=0&edition=prelim), [§168(k)(1)–(2)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title26-section168&num=0&edition=prelim).

The §179 deduction cannot exceed the applicable taxable income from active trades/businesses; amounts disallowed by that income limit can carry forward. Bonus does not have that §179 income limit, although other loss limitations can affect the return. [§179(b)(3)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title26-section179&num=0&edition=prelim), [§168(k)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title26-section168&num=0&edition=prelim).

**E. Bonus has separate qualifications.** It generally includes eligible new property and qualifying used acquisitions, recovery periods of 20 years or less, and the applicable acquisition cohort. Class-level elections out and ADS exclusions matter. The SUV’s §179 cap is not a cap on otherwise eligible bonus depreciation. Thus an eligible heavy SUV can sometimes have its entire **business basis** deducted in year one. This is a conditional application of §§179 and 168, not a rule that any vehicle over 6,000 pounds is fully deductible. [§168(k)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title26-section168&num=0&edition=prelim), [Notice 2026-11](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-26-11.pdf).

Check for same-year disposition or conversion before applying bonus: property placed in service and disposed of in the same tax year is excluded, as is property converted from business to personal use in the same tax year acquired. These exclusions are separate from the potential recapture consequences of a later-year use change. [Publication 946, Excepted Property](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p946).

Prior personal-use conversions do not qualify merely by becoming business property for §179; depreciation can begin at conversion and bonus needs a separate analysis. Do not categorically reject all bonus on conversions or assume purchase-price basis. [Publication 463, Section 179 Deduction](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p463), [Publication 946, Excepted Property](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p946).

### Mileage versus actual expenses

For owned vehicles, standard mileage must be chosen in the first business-use year to retain that method. Prior §179, bonus, or MACRS/other prohibited depreciation prevents standard mileage. A chosen lease mileage method continues for the lease including renewals. Standard mileage and actual vehicle operating costs cannot both be deducted for the same miles; business parking/tolls may be additional. A later switch from mileage to actual expenses uses the applicable straight-line rules. Compare methods only after eligibility is established. [IRS Topic 510](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc510).

Commuting normally is personal. A home-office principal-place-of-business exception or temporary-work-location rule needs facts, not an assumption that every trip from home is business. [Publication 463, Transportation](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p463).

### Recapture

For listed property, a later drop to 50% or less qualified business use can require inclusion of excess depreciation in income and a switch to ADS. Excess depreciation can include §179 and bonus. Selling a depreciated vehicle also requires a disposition/basis/recapture analysis; an upfront deduction does not eliminate later consequences. Do not compute a recapture amount without prior returns, annual use, and depreciation history. [§280F(b)(2)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title26-section280F&num=0&edition=prelim), [Publication 463, Excess Depreciation](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p463).

## 4. Home-office branch (§280A)

Generally require exclusive and regular use as a principal place of business, a qualifying client/patient/customer meeting place, or a qualifying separate structure. Administrative/management activity can establish a principal place if there is no other fixed location for substantial such activity. Inventory storage and daycare have specific exceptions to exclusivity. Obtain area, actual use, other locations, eligible costs, and income limitations. A desk in a shared family room does not automatically qualify. [§280A(c)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title26-section280A&num=0&edition=prelim).

The simplified method is generally $5 per eligible square foot, up to 300 square feet, subject to eligibility and income limits. It is not an automatic $1,500 deduction, and actual-method carryovers need separate handling. [Publication 587](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p587).

## 6. Decision scenarios for evaluation

The expectations below apply the authorities above and test product behavior; they are not additional legal rules.

| Scenario | Expected response |
|---|---|
| Exterior photo of a large SUV; no use details | Ask for exact vehicle/rating and business facts. No asserted GVWR or full-deduction percentage. |
| Truck/van label says exactly 6,000 lb | Do not take the over-6,000 branch; check passenger-auto definition and exceptions. |
| Sedan has 7,000 lb GVWR but unknown unloaded weight | Do not use GVWR alone to remove passenger-auto caps. |
| Ordinary heavy SUV, 50% qualified business use | No §179 or bonus through the ordinary listed-vehicle branch; business operating expenses/straight-line depreciation may qualify. |
| $90,000 eligible heavy SUV, 100% business, bought and in service in 2026, adequate income, no other §179 property/elections | §179 alone is capped at $32,000; qualifying bonus may deduct the remaining business basis. Do not call $90,000 tax savings. |
| Same eligible vehicle, 80% business use | Full purchase price is not deductible. Business-basis ceiling is $72,000 before other basis adjustments; do not add personal use. |
| Pickup with a claimed six-foot bed | Verify interior length, access, and statutory configuration before excluding it from the SUV cap. |
| Purchase/deposit December 2026, ready for business January 2027 | No 2026 placed-in-service deduction merely from payment. 2027 annual limits remain unpublished in this source snapshot. |
| Pre-January 20, 2025 binding acquisition, delivery 2026 | Review acquisition rules; do not automatically apply 100% bonus. |
| §179/bonus claimed previously; user requests standard mileage | Flag method incompatibility and obtain history. |
| 1,000 eligible miles in June 2026 and 1,000 in July | $725 + $760 = $1,485 before separate qualifying parking/tolls; not $1,450 or $1,520. |
| Prior accelerated deduction, 40% business use next year | Trigger potential recapture and ADS review; do not promise zero future consequences. |
| General office supplies with receipt and stated business purpose | Potential current ordinary/necessary deduction after checking personal allocation and reimbursement. |
| Client entertainment tickets with business conversation | Generally nondeductible entertainment; business discussion does not override §274. |
| Shared dining table used for occasional admin work | Do not approve a regular/exclusive-use home office without a qualifying exception. |
| User asks exact 2027 SUV cap or mileage rate | State annual amount not yet published as of September 15, 2026; do not substitute 2026. |
