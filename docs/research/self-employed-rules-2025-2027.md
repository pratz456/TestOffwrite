### 1.2 When the standard mileage rate is allowed (Pub 463 (2025), ch. 4 — VERIFIED)
Source: https://www.irs.gov/publications/p463
- Must elect standard mileage in the **first year** the car is available for business use; thereafter may switch between standard and actual each year (owned car). Switching to actual after standard requires straight-line depreciation for the car's remaining estimated life and basis reduction by the depreciation component above.
- **Leased** car: if standard mileage is used, it must be used for the **entire lease period** (including renewals).
- Standard mileage is **not allowed** if the taxpayer: (a) operates **five or more cars** at the same time (fleet); (b) claimed depreciation other than straight-line on the car; (c) claimed a **§179** deduction or special (bonus) depreciation on the car; (d) claimed actual car expenses after 1997 for a leased car.
- Standard mileage replaces depreciation, lease payments, maintenance/repairs, gas, oil, insurance, registration. **Still separately deductible** with standard mileage: business-portion parking fees and tolls; for self-employed, business portion of car-loan interest and business portion of personal property tax (Pub 463 ch. 4; Pub 334 ch. 8).
- Actual expense method: allocate all operating costs and depreciation by business-use percentage (business miles ÷ total miles). Vehicle records required (see §10).
- Commuting is nondeductible personal use (Rev. Rul. 99-7; Pub 463 ch. 4).

## 2. Meals, entertainment, and travel per diem

### 2.1 Meals (VERIFIED — IRC §274(k),(n); Pub 463 (2025) ch. 2; Pub 334 (2025) ch. 8)
- General rule: business meals deductible at **50%** (§274(n)(1)) if (i) not lavish or extravagant and (ii) the taxpayer (or an employee) is present (§274(k)). Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/274 ; https://www.irs.gov/publications/p463
- The 100% "restaurant" allowance (Consolidated Appropriations Act 2021) applied only to 2021–2022 and has **expired**; 2025–2027 meals are 50% (Pub 334 (2025) "Business meal expense. Deductions ... generally remain limited to 50%").
- **80%** for food/beverages consumed while away from home during, or incident to, a period of duty subject to **DOT hours-of-service** limits (§274(n)(3)) — e.g., interstate truck/bus drivers, certain rail, air, and merchant-marine crews.
- Exceptions to the 50% cut (§274(n)(2), §274(e)): meals treated as compensation to the recipient (W-2/1099); recreational/social expenses primarily for non-highly-compensated employees (e.g., holiday party); items available to the public / sold to customers for adequate consideration; de minimis fringe items (§274(n)(2) after 2025 — see next bullet).
- OBBBA/TCJA sunset: after **Dec 31, 2025**, employer-provided meals excludible under §119 and §132(e)(1) de minimis (including employer eating facilities) become **nondeductible** under §274(o), except certain exceptions (e.g., restaurants' employee meals, certain fishing vessels/processing facilities per OBBBA). Source: §274(o) text at Cornell LII (above). Impact on Schedule C filers is limited to those providing meals to employees.
- Per-diem (standard meal allowance) method for travel meals: self-employed may use the federal **M&IE rate** in lieu of actual meal cost; **lodging must be actual** (no lodging per diem for self-employed/owners — Rev. Proc. 2019-48 §4.02, §6.05; Pub 463 ch. 1 "There is no optional standard lodging amount"). The 50% limit still applies to the M&IE amount. Records of time, place, business purpose still required.
- Partial travel days: prorate M&IE at ¾ of the daily rate for departure/return days (Method 1) or any consistent reasonable method (Method 2) (Pub 463 ch. 1).
- Incidental-expenses-only rate: **$5/day** (when no meal expense incurred; not subject to 50%). Source: Notice 2025-54 §4; Pub 463.

### 2.2 Entertainment (VERIFIED)
- **Nondeductible** (§274(a), as amended by TCJA, permanent): entertainment, amusement, recreation, club dues, entertainment facilities. Food/beverages provided at an entertainment event are 50% deductible only if purchased separately or separately stated on the invoice (Reg. §1.274-11(d)(2) Ex. 2; Pub 463 ch. 2).
- Business gifts: $25 per recipient per year (§274(b)); incidental costs (engraving, wrapping, shipping) excluded from the $25 (Pub 463 ch. 3).

## 3. Home office deduction (§280A; Pub 587 (2025); Rev. Proc. 2013-13; Form 8829) — VERIFIED
Sources: https://www.irs.gov/publications/p587 ; https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-13-13.pdf ; https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc509 ; https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/faqs-simplified-method-for-home-office-deduction

Qualifying use tests (all years 2025–2027; no OBBBA change):
- Space used **exclusively and regularly** as (a) the **principal place of business** for any trade or business, (b) a place to meet patients/clients/customers in the normal course of business, or (c) a separate unattached structure used in connection with the business (§280A(c)(1)).
- "Principal place of business" includes a home office used for **administrative or management activities** if there is no other fixed location where the taxpayer conducts substantial administrative/management activities (§280A(c)(1) flush language; Pub 587).
- Exceptions to the exclusive-use test: (i) **storage of inventory or product samples** (regular use; home must be the sole fixed location of the business), (ii) licensed **daycare** facility (regular use; deduction prorated by hours).
- Employees: no deduction 2018–2025 (TCJA) and permanently after OBBBA §70110 (misc. itemized deductions eliminated). Schedule C filers unaffected.

Simplified (safe-harbor) method — Rev. Proc. 2013-13:
- **$5 per square foot × up to 300 sq ft = maximum $1,500** per year. Rate unchanged since 2013; IRS "may update this rate from time to time."
- Elected annually on Schedule C (lines for total home sq ft and business sq ft); **no Form 8829**. May switch between simplified and actual from year to year (irrevocable for the year once elected).
- **Depreciation deemed zero** for years the simplified method is used; no depreciation recapture generated for those years. If later switching to actual, use the MACRS optional table for the applicable year.
- **No carryover**: deduction is limited to gross income from the business use minus other business expenses; the disallowed excess cannot be carried forward (and prior-year actual-method carryovers cannot be used in a simplified year).
- Mortgage interest, real estate taxes, casualty losses on the home are claimed in full on Schedule A (if itemizing); no allocation.
- Part-year / changing area: average monthly allowable square footage; count a month only if ≥15 days of qualified use; ≤300 sq ft per month.
- Spouses may each use simplified for separate portions of the same home (each capped at 300 sq ft).
- Daycare non-exclusive use: reduce the $5 rate by hours-used ÷ hours-available.

Actual-expense (regular) method — Form 8829:
- Business percentage = business area ÷ total home area (or rooms, if similar size). **Direct** expenses (painting the office) 100%; **indirect** expenses (mortgage interest, taxes, insurance, utilities, repairs, rent, depreciation) × business %.
- **Depreciation** of the business portion of an owned home: nonresidential real property, 39-year straight-line, mid-month convention (Pub 587 "Depreciating Your Home"; Pub 946). Depreciation allowed/allowable is recaptured as unrecaptured §1250 gain (25% max) on sale and is not excludable under §121.
- **Gross-income limitation** (§280A(c)(5)): deduction ≤ gross income from business use of the home minus (business expenses not related to the home) minus (allocable mortgage interest/taxes/casualty). Expenses in excess **carry forward** indefinitely to later years the actual method is used (Form 8829 Part IV).
- Order of deduction: (1) home mortgage interest, taxes, casualty losses (allowed regardless); (2) operating expenses (insurance, utilities, repairs); (3) depreciation last.
- Records: keep proof of the home's basis, improvements, area measurements, and all indirect expense bills.

### 4.2 Bonus (additional first-year) depreciation §168(k) — VERIFIED
Sources: Notice 2026-11 https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-26-11.pdf ; IRS news release https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/treasury-irs-issue-guidance-on-the-additional-first-year-depreciation-deduction-amended-as-part-of-the-one-big-beautiful-bill
- OBBBA §70301: **permanent 100%** bonus depreciation for qualified property **acquired after Jan 19, 2025 and placed in service after Jan 19, 2025** (no sunset).
- Acquisition-date test: apply Reg. §1.168(k)-2 by substituting "January 19, 2025" for "September 27, 2017" (written binding contract rule applies — property under a binding contract signed on or before Jan 19, 2025 is treated as acquired before Jan 20, 2025).
- Property acquired **before Jan 20, 2025** stays on the TCJA phase-down: **40%** if placed in service in 2025 (60% for long-production-period property/aircraft); **20%** if placed in service in 2026; 0% in 2027 (former §168(k)(6)). Notice 2026-11 §2.01 confirms the 40% 2025 figure; 2026/2027 phase-down figures per former §168(k)(6) — VERIFIED by statute (prior law).
- Transition election (§168(k)(10) as amended): for the **first tax year ending after Jan 19, 2025**, taxpayer may elect 40% (60% for LPPP/aircraft) instead of 100%.
- Eligible: MACRS property with recovery period ≤20 years, computer software, qualified improvement property, certain plants; new or **used** property (original-use or "first use by taxpayer" rules). Election out is by asset class per year.
- New "qualified production property" (§168(n)) 100% expensing for certain manufacturing real property (construction beginning after Jan 19, 2025 and before Jan 1, 2029) — rarely relevant to Schedule C.

### 4.4 Listed property (§280F(d)(4)) — VERIFIED (Pub 946 ch. 5; §280F)
- Listed property = passenger automobiles; other property used for transportation; property generally used for entertainment/recreation/amusement. **Computers and peripherals were removed** from listed property for property placed in service after 2017 (TCJA).

### 4.5 De minimis safe harbor (Reg. §1.263(a)-1(f)) — VERIFIED
Sources: Notice 2015-82 https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-15-82.pdf ; IRS tangible property FAQ https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/tangible-property-final-regulations
- **$2,500 per invoice or per item** (as substantiated by the invoice) for taxpayers **without** an applicable financial statement (AFS) — effective tax years beginning on/after Jan 1, 2016; still in effect 2025–2027 (no change).
- **$5,000** per invoice/item for taxpayers **with** an AFS and written accounting procedures.
- Requirements: accounting procedures in place at the beginning of the year treating such amounts as expenses (written procedures required only for AFS taxpayers); amounts expensed on books; **annual election statement** titled "Section 1.263(a)-1(f) de minimis safe harbor election" attached to a timely filed original return (including extensions); applies to all qualifying expenditures in the year; cannot be made on an amended return; does not apply to inventory, land, or amounts paid for property under §263A that must be capitalized to inventory.
- Amounts above the threshold: capitalize and depreciate (or §179/bonus). Materials and supplies rule (Reg. §1.162-3): items ≤$200 or useful life ≤12 months deductible when used/consumed.

## 5. Self-employed health insurance, retirement plans, HSAs, IRAs

### 5.1 Self-employed health insurance deduction (§162(l); Form 7206) — VERIFIED
Sources: Form 7206 & Instructions (2025) https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i7206 ; https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/f7206.pdf ; 2026 draft instructions https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-dft/i7206--dft.pdf
- Above-the-line deduction (Schedule 1, line 17) for medical, dental, vision, qualified long-term-care premiums, and **Medicare premiums** (Parts A (if voluntary), B, C, D, Medigap) for the taxpayer, spouse, dependents, and children under age 27 at year end.
- Plan must be **established under the business** (for a sole proprietor, policy can be in the individual's name; premiums paid personally).
- **Limit**: cannot exceed net profit from the business (Schedule C line 31) minus the deductible half of SE tax minus self-employed SEP/SIMPLE/qualified plan contributions attributable to that business (Form 7206 lines 4–13). Cannot create a loss. Excess premiums may go to Schedule A medical (subject to 7.5% AGI floor).
- **Disqualifying month**: no deduction for any month the taxpayer was **eligible** to participate in a subsidized health plan of an employer of the taxpayer, spouse, dependent, or child under 27 — even if not enrolled. Applied separately to LTC vs. non-LTC plans.
- Does **not** reduce net earnings for SE tax (Schedule SE); does reduce QBI (Reg. §1.199A-3(b)(1)(vi)).
- LTC premium age-based caps included in the deduction: **2025**: $480 (≤40), $900 (41–50), $1,800 (51–60), $4,810 (61–70), $6,020 (71+) — Form 7206 (2025). **2026**: $500, $930, $1,860, $4,960, $6,200 — Rev. Proc. 2025-32 §4.27. 2027: UNVERIFIED.
- Premium Tax Credit interaction: iterative calculation (Rev. Proc. 2014-41; Pub 974) when Marketplace coverage with APTC; only premiums not covered by the PTC are deductible.

The 25% / 20% rule (Pub 560 ch. 2, 5 — VERIFIED):
- Employer contribution limit for a SEP or profit-sharing plan is **25% of compensation** for common-law employees.
- For the **self-employed owner**, "compensation" = net earnings from self-employment (Schedule C net profit) **minus the deductible half of SE tax minus the contribution itself**. Because the contribution reduces its own base, the effective maximum is **20% of (net profit − ½ SE tax)** (Pub 560 Rate Table: plan rate 25% → self-employed rate 0.20). Use Pub 560 "Rate Worksheet/Deduction Worksheet for Self-Employed."
- Solo 401(k): owner may also make elective deferrals up to $23,500 (2025) / $24,500 (2026) plus catch-up, **in addition to** the 20%-of-adjusted-net-earnings employer contribution, total capped at §415(c) ($70,000 / $72,000, plus catch-up on top). Deferral election must be made by Dec 31 (deferrals themselves can be deposited by the return due date incl. extensions); a sole proprietor with no employees may adopt a new 401(k) after year-end up to the unextended due date and treat first-year deferrals as timely if paid before the unextended due date (SECURE 2.0 §317; Pub 560).
- SEP: may be **established and funded** up to the return due date **including extensions**; no catch-up in SEP.
- Retirement deduction is on Schedule 1 (line 16), does not reduce SE tax, does reduce QBI.
- Form 5500-EZ required once one-participant plan assets exceed $250,000 (or in final year).

## 6. Qualified Business Income deduction (§199A) for sole proprietors — VERIFIED
Sources: 26 U.S.C. §199A (as amended by OBBBA §70105) https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title26-section199A&num=0&edition=prelim ; Rev. Proc. 2025-32 §4.26 ; 2025 Form 8995-A & Instructions https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/i8995a.pdf ; 2026 Form 1040-ES "What's New" https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-prior/f1040es--2026.pdf

Computation (unchanged mechanics):
1. **QBI** of a Schedule C business = net profit (Schedule C line 31) **reduced by** the deductible half of SE tax, the SEHI deduction, and self-employed retirement contributions attributable to the business (Reg. §1.199A-3(b)(1)(vi)); excludes capital gains/losses, interest not allocable to the business, wages, guaranteed payments, and (2025+) amounts deducted as **qualified tips under §224** (2025 Form 8995-A instr.). Negative QBI carries forward and offsets future QBI.
2. **Tentative deduction** = 20% of QBI (per business, then combined) + 20% of qualified REIT dividends/PTP income.
3. **Overall limit** = 20% × (taxable income before QBI deduction − net capital gain).
4. **Wage/property limit** (applies only above the threshold): per business, QBI component limited to the greater of (a) 50% of W-2 wages paid by the business, or (b) 25% of W-2 wages + 2.5% of UBIA of qualified property. A sole proprietor with **no employees** has $0 W-2 wages, so above the phase-in range the deduction is limited to 2.5% of UBIA (unadjusted basis of depreciable property, held ≥ the greater of 10 years or its recovery period).
5. **SSTB** (specified service trade or business, §199A(d)(2)): health, law, accounting, actuarial science, performing arts, consulting, athletics, financial services, brokerage, investing/investment management, trading, dealing in securities/partnership interests/commodities, or any business whose principal asset is the **reputation or skill** of its owner/employees (narrowly defined in Reg. §1.199A-5(b)(2)(xiv): endorsements, licensing of likeness, appearance fees). SSTB income: full deduction below threshold; phased out across the range; **zero** above the range. De minimis rule: a business with gross receipts ≤$25M is not an SSTB if <10% of receipts are from SSTB services (<5% if >$25M).
6. Forms: Form 8995 (taxable income ≤ threshold, no SSTB complications) or Form 8995-A.
7. The QBI deduction reduces **income tax only**; it does not reduce SE tax, NIIT, or Additional Medicare Tax.

## 10. Recordkeeping and substantiation standards — VERIFIED
Sources: Reg. §1.274-5 and §1.274-5T https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.274-5 , https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.274-5T ; Pub 463 (2025) ch. 5 ; Pub 583 (Starting a Business and Keeping Records) ; IRC §274(d), §6001

- **General rule (§6001; Reg. §1.6001-1)**: keep records sufficient to establish income, deductions, credits. Bank/credit card statements plus invoices/receipts; a cancelled check alone does not prove business purpose (Reg. §1.274-5(c)(2)(iii)).
- **Cohan rule** (Cohan v. Commissioner, 39 F.2d 540 (2d Cir. 1930)): courts may **estimate** a deductible amount when the taxpayer proves an expense was incurred but cannot prove the exact amount — the estimate "bears heavily" against the taxpayer. **Limits**: §274(d) **overrides Cohan** for (1) travel away from home (including meals and lodging while traveling), (2) gifts, and (3) **listed property** (vehicles) — for these, **no deduction** is allowed without adequate records or sufficient corroborating evidence of each element; estimates are prohibited (Reg. §1.274-5T(a)). Courts also decline Cohan estimates where the taxpayer offers no rational basis at all (e.g., Vanicek v. Commissioner, 85 T.C. 731 (1985)).
- **Elements** to substantiate (§274(d); Reg. §1.274-5T(b)): amount; time (dates; for vehicles, date of use); place (destination; for vehicles, mileage per use and total annual mileage); business purpose; and for gifts, the business relationship of the recipient.
- **$75 receipt rule** (Reg. §1.274-5(c)(2)(iii)): documentary evidence (receipt, paid bill) is **required for all lodging** regardless of amount and for **any other expenditure of $75 or more** (except transportation charges where receipts are not readily available). Below $75 (non-lodging), a receipt is not required, but the log/diary entries for amount, time, place, and purpose still are. Employers may set stricter policies.
- **Adequate records** = an account book, diary, log, statement of expense, trip sheet, or similar record **made at or near the time** of the expenditure/use, plus documentary evidence (Reg. §1.274-5T(c)(2)). A **contemporaneous log is not legally mandatory**, but a record made at or near the time has "a high degree of credibility," and reconstructed statements require corroborating evidence with "a high degree of probative value" (Reg. §1.274-5T(c)(1)). Electronic apps/GPS logs qualify if they capture the elements. Weekly recording is treated as at-or-near-time (Reg. §1.274-5T(c)(2)(ii)(A)).
- **Vehicles (listed property)**: record for **each business use** the date, mileage, destination, and purpose, plus total miles for the year (odometer readings at start/end of year). **Sampling** is allowed if the sample period is representative (e.g., first week of each month) and the pattern is documented (Reg. §1.274-5T(c)(3)(ii)). Schedule C Part IV / Form 4562 Part V ask for total, business, commuting, and other miles and whether written evidence exists.
- Meals: retain receipts showing restaurant name/location, date, amount, number of people; record attendees and business purpose (Pub 463 ch. 5 Table 5-1). Since 2018, local business meals are outside §274(d)'s strict list but the IRS still requires these elements under §162/§274(k) and Reg. §1.274-12.
- Home office: retain measurements/floor plan, utility bills, insurance, mortgage statements, and depreciation schedules.
- **Retention** (Pub 583): generally **3 years** from filing (or due date); **6 years** if income understated by >25%; **7 years** for bad-debt/worthless-security claims; **indefinitely** if no return/fraudulent return; **employment tax records 4 years**; asset records until the limitations period expires for the year the asset is disposed of. Records establishing basis (home, vehicles, equipment) must be kept as long as the asset is held plus the limitations period.
- Per diem method still requires proof of time, place, and business purpose of travel (Pub 463 ch. 1).

## 11. OBBBA (P.L. 119-21, enacted July 4, 2025) provisions affecting self-employed individuals

### 11.1 Deduction for qualified tips (§224; OBBBA §70201) — VERIFIED
Sources: Final regs TD 10044, 91 FR 19026 (Apr 13, 2026; effective Jun 12, 2026) https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-04-13/pdf/2026-07104.pdf ; IRS release https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/treasury-irs-issue-final-regulations-listing-occupations-where-workers-customarily-and-regularly-receive-tips-under-the-one-big-beautiful-bill ; Proposed regs REG-110032-25, 90 FR 45340 (Sept 22, 2025) https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/09/22/2025-18278/occupations-that-customarily-and-regularly-received-tips-definition-of-qualified-tips ; Notice 2025-69 (2025 transition guidance) https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-69.pdf ; Notice 2025-62 (2025 reporting penalty relief); IRS list https://www.irs.gov/TippedOccupations
- **Years**: tax years beginning after Dec 31, 2024 and before Jan 1, 2029 (**2025–2028**). Claimed on **Schedule 1-A (Form 1040)**; available to itemizers and non-itemizers; reduces taxable income but **not** AGI, and **not** SE tax.
- **Cap**: **$25,000 per return** regardless of filing status (proposed/final regs). MAGI phase-out: reduced by **$100 for every $1,000** (or fraction) of MAGI over **$150,000** ($300,000 joint) — fully gone at $400,000 / $550,000. Phase-out applied after the $25,000 cap.
- **Self-employed net-income cap** (§224(c)): deduction cannot exceed the **gross income (including the tips) from the trade or business in which the tips were received minus the allocable deductions** of that business (i.e., net profit of that Schedule C, computed without the §224 deduction). No deduction if the business has a loss.
- **SSTB exclusion** (§224(d)(2)(B)): tips received in the course of a **§199A(d)(2) SSTB** are not qualified tips. For self-employed individuals the test is applied to the individual's own trade or business (e.g., a self-employed comedian's tips are performing-arts SSTB income → **not** qualified; a pianist employed by a non-SSTB hotel → qualified). Notice 2025-69 grants employees (not the self-employed) enforcement transition relief on the SSTB test until Jan 1 of the year after SSTB final regs issue.
- **Occupation test**: must be in an occupation on Treasury's List of Occupations that Receive Tips — **71 Treasury Tipped Occupation Codes (TTOCs)** in 8 categories (100s beverage & food service; 200s entertainment & events; 300s hospitality & guest services; 400s home services; 500s personal services; 600s personal appearance & wellness; 700s recreation & instruction; 800s transportation & delivery), per Reg. §1.224-1 Table 1. Final rule added codes 509 Visual Artists, 510 Floral Designers, 810 Gas Pump Attendants. Rideshare/delivery drivers are included (800s).
- **"Qualified tip" definition**: cash or cash-equivalent (card, app, tip-sharing) paid **voluntarily**, not negotiated, amount determined by the payor; **excludes** mandatory service charges/auto-gratuities, and tips received in an illegal activity, prostitution, or pornographic activity. Tips must appear on Form W-2 (box 14b TTOC), 1099-NEC, 1099-MISC, 1099-K, or be reported on Form 4137 (employees). Married taxpayers must file jointly; valid SSN required.
- **2025 transition (Notice 2025-69)**: because 2025 information returns were not required to break out tips, non-employees may compute qualified tips using a reasonable method from earnings statements, POS reports, tip logs, or TPSO records, and use them in figuring the deduction.
- Tips deducted under §224 are excluded from QBI (2025 Form 8995-A instr.) and still count for SE tax.
- The parallel **overtime deduction** (§225, $12,500/$25,000) applies only to FLSA §7 overtime paid to employees — not available for self-employment income.

### 11.2 Car loan interest deduction (§163(h)(4); OBBBA §70203) — VERIFIED
Sources: IRS https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/one-big-beautiful-bill-provisions-individuals-and-workers ; https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/working-families-tax-cuts-tax-deductions-for-working-americans-and-seniors ; Topic 505 https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc505 ; Final regs (TD, Federal Register Sept 8, 2026; effective Nov 9, 2026) https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/09/08/2026-18219/car-loan-interest-deduction ; Notice 2025-57 (transitional lender reporting relief)
- **Years 2025–2028.** Up to **$10,000/year** of "qualified passenger vehicle loan interest" (QPVLI); above-the-line-style deduction available to itemizers and non-itemizers (Schedule 1-A). Phase-out: reduced by **$200 per $1,000** of MAGI over **$100,000** ($200,000 joint) — fully gone at $150,000 / $250,000.
- Loan requirements: originated **after Dec 31, 2024**; used to purchase a vehicle whose **original use begins with the taxpayer** (new vehicle; used vehicles excluded); secured by a **first lien** on the vehicle; **personal-use** vehicle; leases excluded; refinancing allowed up to the refinanced balance; related-party loans excluded; negative equity rolled in is excluded (final regs; pro-rata allocation required for non-vehicle items financed).

- **Interaction with Schedule C (mixed-use vehicle)**: interest allocable to business use remains deductible as trade-or-business interest under §163(a) (Schedule C line 16b, business-use %); the personal-use remainder may be QPVLI (subject to $10,000/MAGI limits). Preamble example: $3,500 interest, 45% business → $1,575 on Schedule C and $1,925 as QPVLI; alternatively all $3,500 as QPVLI. **No double deduction** of the same interest. If the vehicle was expected to be **>50% business** when purchased, **none** of the interest is QPVLI (only the business portion is deductible, on Schedule C).
