# WriteOff Written Information Security Program (WISP)

Date: 2026-09-21. Version: 1.2 (dependency-remediation update the same day against commit 1405212; v1.1 re-verified the other controls against release candidate 36c3217 and the live project `writeoff-23910`; v1.0 dated 2026-09-17). Owner: Qualified Individual (see §2). Review: at least annually and after any material change.

Basis: FTC Standards for Safeguarding Customer Information, 16 CFR Part 314 (as amended 2021; breach notification added 2023, effective May 2024). Tax preparation firms are listed as "financial institutions" in §314.2(h); a person who develops software used to prepare or file returns is a "tax return preparer" under Treas. Reg. §301.7216-1(b)(2)(i)(B). IRS Pub 4557 and Pub 5708 (WISP template) were used as checklists. Every control below is one that exists in the repository at commit 1405212 (release candidate) unless it is listed under "Gaps and owners"; controls that depend on the deployed release are marked as such. Claims that could not be verified from code or a primary source are marked unverified.

Small-institution note: §314.6 exempts institutions that maintain customer information on fewer than 5,000 consumers from some program elements (FTC compliance guide). The exact list of exempted paragraphs was not verified against the eCFR text in this pass (site blocked automated retrieval); WriteOff adopts all nine elements regardless, and the breach-notification duty in §314.4(j) applies at any size.

## 1. Scope and objectives (§314.3)

Customer information in scope: everything a user gives WriteOff or that WriteOff derives to analyze self-employment deductions: login identity, profile and business facts, bank account and transaction data obtained through Plaid, receipt images and extracted text, tax organizer answers (including SSNs), imported W-2/1099 data, AI analysis outputs, billing status, support requests, and operational logs. Objectives: confidentiality and integrity of that information, protection against anticipated threats, and prevention of unauthorized access that could cause substantial harm to a user.

Systems in scope: Firebase project `writeoff-23910` (Hosting, Cloud Run SSR service `ssrwriteoff23910` in `us-central1`, Firestore, Cloud Storage, Firebase Authentication, Cloud Functions `functions` and `functions-analysis` in `us-central1`), the GitHub repository and Actions runners, operator workstations, and the vendor services listed in `docs/compliance/SUBPROCESSORS_2026-09-17.md`.

## 2. Qualified Individual (§314.4(a))

Role: one named person accountable for implementing and supervising this program, approving encryption alternatives and MFA equivalents in writing, receiving incident reports, and reporting at least annually to the owner (§9). May be an employee or a contractor; if a contractor, WriteOff still designates a senior person to oversee them.

Status: designated on 2026-09-21 (the founder). The name, date and contact are recorded in the operator-held appendix to this document, not in the public repository; the appendix accompanies copies of this program shared with vendors.

### 3.2 Foreseeable threats and current controls

| Threat | Controls in place | Residual risk |
|---|---|---|
| Credential stuffing / phishing of users | Email verification required before API access; IP-scoped session-creation limit (30 per 10 min); Firebase Auth password policy (Google-managed, configuration unverified) | High: no MFA for users (G2) |
| Compromise of an operator Google or GitHub account | Deploy requires manual `workflow_dispatch` with the exact 40-character commit and a typed confirmation. Project IAM verified 2026-09-21: three human principals (one Owner, two Editors) and six service accounts; no other humans hold project access | High: MFA on those accounts is operator-configured, unverified (G2); the two Editor grants exceed least privilege (G5) |
| Support-tool misuse | `SUPPORT_ADMIN_UIDS` allowlist AND `admin` custom claim; redacted diagnostics only (forbidden keys: token, secret, ssn, cursor, amount, password, private, encrypted); every lookup written to `support_audit`; 60 lookups per 10 min | Medium: raw Firestore console access by project IAM members is not logged at the application layer (G6) |
| Bank-token theft from a database export | Tokens are ciphertext without `PLAID_TOKEN_ENCRYPTION_KEY`; key held in environment/Secret Manager, never in Firestore. The release candidate never stores a bank token in a profile document; the deployed release's legacy plaintext tokens are moved into the encrypted store on the account's first profile read after cutover | Low once the key is provisioned (generated 2026-09-21, provisioning pending); no documented rotation (G4) |
| Leakage of identifiers to the AI vendor | `store: false` on every OpenAI call; every string in the transaction-analysis payload is passed through the shared identifier redactor (`redactIdentifierStrings`: SSN/ITIN/EIN shapes, bare nine-digit runs including after an OCR-misread colon; amounts preserved), so new free-text fields are covered by default; no SSN field is sent from transaction analysis. W-2/1099/platform-summary imports are read by local Tesseract OCR first and only the text, with SSN/ITIN/EIN-shaped numbers replaced by `[redacted-id]`, goes to the model (`lib/ocr/document-text.ts`, `lib/security/identifier-redaction.ts`); the whole image is sent only under a signed §7216 consent (`consents.document_import`) plus a per-document authorization, else 403 `DOCUMENT_IMAGE_CONSENT_REQUIRED` | Medium: bank/credit-card statement images (`import-bank-statement`) are still sent whole to GPT-4o vision (G3) |
| Firestore rules misconfiguration | Rules enforce UID ownership; server-only collections deny all client access; 18 emulator rule tests in `tests/security-rules.emulator.test.ts` | Low |
| Cross-site request forgery | `lib/security/request-origin.ts` rejects requests whose Origin/Referer is not a trusted application host; SameSite cookies | Low |
| Malicious uploads | 10 MB limit, content-type and byte-signature checks, UID-scoped Storage rules | Low |
| Key or secret exposure in code | Secrets are read from environment; CI does not print them; release scripts refuse to write private outputs inside the checkout | Medium: no secret scanning configured in the repository (unverified whether GitHub secret scanning is enabled) |
| Accidental deletion or corrupt writes | Deletion is transactional and refuses to finish when any cleanup step fails | Medium: verified 2026-09-21 that the production database has point-in-time recovery DISABLED, delete protection DISABLED and no backup schedule; the observability kit (`scripts/production-observability.sh --apply`) enables PITR, a daily backup schedule and weekly exports (G7) |

Reassess this table when a new data class, vendor, or region is added, and at least annually.

## 4. Safeguards (§314.4(c)) mapped to actual controls

1. Access controls (§314.4(c)(1)). Authentication is Firebase Auth (email/password with mandatory email verification, or Google sign-in). Every API route calls `getAuthenticatedUser`, which verifies a Firebase ID token or the `__session` cookie with revocation checking. Authorization is UID ownership in Firestore and Storage rules; `plaid_connections`, `account_deletions`, `rate_limits`, and `support_audit` are server-only. Support administrators must be both allowlisted in `SUPPORT_ADMIN_UIDS` and carry `admin: true`. Periodic access review: none documented (G5).
2. Data and system inventory (§314.4(c)(2)). §3.1 above and `docs/compliance/SUBPROCESSORS_2026-09-17.md`. Update both when the schema or vendor set changes.
3. Encryption (§314.4(c)(3)). In transit: HTTPS everywhere; verified 2026-09-21 against `writeoffapp.com` that TLS 1.0 and 1.1 are refused, TLS 1.2 is the floor and TLS 1.3 is negotiated by default; `middleware.ts` sets HSTS (`max-age=31536000; includeSubDomains; preload`), a Content-Security-Policy, X-Frame-Options, Referrer-Policy and Permissions-Policy. Server-to-vendor calls (Plaid, Stripe, OpenAI, Resend) use HTTPS from Node 22, whose default minimum is TLS 1.2. At rest: Google default encryption for Firestore, Storage, Auth and logs; application-layer AES-256-GCM for Plaid access tokens and for every organizer identifier answer (SSNs, bank account and routing numbers, IP PIN, EIN, explicit dependent identifiers); identifier digits are removed from dependent free text before storage. Not encrypted at field level: profile EIN, W-2/1099 fields including employer/payer EIN (G3). Keys: `PLAID_TOKEN_ENCRYPTION_KEY` and `SSN_ENCRYPTION_KEY` are distinct 32-byte values held outside the repository (`docs/PRODUCTION_CUTOVER_2026-09-16.md`). Verified 2026-09-21: the deployed release has no `SSN_ENCRYPTION_KEY` and the production database holds no organizer identifiers, so the keys generated on 2026-09-21 become the production keys at cutover with nothing to re-encrypt; no rotation or escrow procedure exists yet (G4).
4. Secure development (§314.4(c)(4)). TypeScript build, ESLint, and a Vitest suite (204 test files / 5,296 tests, including Firestore rules emulator tests and a 137-operation API contract test) run on every push and pull request via `.github/workflows/ci.yml` (`npm run ci` = lint, test, build). `npm run production:preflight` checks project configuration before release and rejects test credentials, emulator flags and the wrong provider account. Dependency audit after remediation on 2026-09-21 (`npm audit --omit=dev` at commit 1405212): 0 critical, 4 high, 14 moderate (18 total). Critical findings were closed the same day (Next.js 15.5.25, js-yaml 4.3.2). The four remaining high findings require major upgrades and are not patched in place: `firebase-frameworks` (and `sharp` through it), `postcss` through a Next.js 16 upgrade, and `serialize-javascript` through `next-pwa`. CI fails the build when a production dependency has a known critical finding (`npm audit --omit=dev --audit-level=critical`). Dependabot opens weekly npm security updates for `/`, `/functions`, and `/functions-analysis`, and monthly GitHub Actions updates. No SAST step, no scan of employee or contractor machines, no penetration test, and no written patch SLA for high and moderate findings (G8).
5. Multi-factor authentication (§314.4(c)(5)). Users: not enforced. The codebase never enrolls or requires a second factor; Firebase Auth MFA requires the Identity Platform upgrade and enrollment code, neither of which is present. The only MFA-aware code reads the `sign_in_second_factor` claim to gate the filing sandbox (`app/api/tax/filing/route.ts`). Support administrators: no second factor required by the application. Operator consoles (Google Cloud, Firebase, GitHub, Stripe, Plaid, OpenAI): MFA is configurable by the operator and is unverified. No written Qualified Individual approval of an equivalent control exists. This is the largest Safeguards Rule gap (G2) and also fails IRS Pub 1345's MFA expectation for online providers should WriteOff transmit returns.
6. Secure disposal (§314.4(c)(6)). User-initiated deletion (`lib/firebase/delete-user-data.ts`) revokes bank access at Plaid, cancels Stripe subscriptions and deletes the Stripe customer, deletes receipt objects, deletes owned Firestore collections and the profile tree, then deletes the Auth user. No automatic disposal of inactive accounts after two years of non-use exists (G10). Schedule: `docs/compliance/DATA_RETENTION_AND_DELETION_2026-09-17.md`.
7. Change management (§314.4(c)(7)). Changes reach production only through `.github/workflows/deploy.yml`, a manual `workflow_dispatch` that checks out the exact reviewed commit and requires typing `deploy:writeoff-23910:<release_commit>`; the release script refuses to deploy from a dirty tree, verifies every file against the reviewed commit's blob ids, requires a signed migration-review record, and refuses any Node major other than 22. Rules and indexes deploy with the release (the composite indexes the release needs were deployed additively on 2026-09-21). Branch protection and mandatory review on `march-branch` are GitHub settings that could not be verified from the repository (unverified).
8. Activity monitoring (§314.4(c)(8)). Support reads are logged to `support_audit`. Scheduled bank syncs write per-user status fields. Application errors go to Cloud Logging through `lib/error-logger.ts`. Firestore/Storage Data Access audit logs and Cloud Monitoring alerts (5xx, function failures, support-audit anomalies) are being defined on a separate branch and are not present here (G6).

## 5. Testing and monitoring (§314.4(d))

Automated: CI test suite on every change; rules emulator tests; a production-dependency audit that fails CI on critical findings; Dependabot on a weekly schedule. Not present: continuous monitoring of the production information system, an annual penetration test, vulnerability scans of employee or contractor machines, and SAST (G8). Until continuous monitoring exists, the Rule's alternative is an annual penetration test plus vulnerability assessments every six months.

## 6. Training (§314.4(e))

No security-awareness training or acknowledgment records exist (G5). Minimum program: annual review of this WISP, the incident response plan, and IRS Pub 4557 by everyone with production or support access; signed acknowledgment kept by the Qualified Individual; specialized training for whoever holds the encryption keys and Firebase admin roles.

## 7. Service provider oversight (§314.4(f))

Vendors, data categories, and agreement links are in `docs/compliance/SUBPROCESSORS_2026-09-17.md`. Required: confirm the Google Cloud, Stripe, Plaid, OpenAI, and Resend data processing terms are accepted under WriteOff's accounts and keep dated copies; review each vendor annually; give any contractor that receives tax return information the written §7216/§6713 notice required by Treas. Reg. §301.7216-2(d)(2) (G11).

## 8. Program maintenance (§314.4(g)) and incident response (§314.4(h))

Review triggers: new data class, new vendor, new region, a security event, a failed test, or a change in law. Incident response: `docs/compliance/INCIDENT_RESPONSE_PLAN_2026-09-17.md`, which includes FTC notification within 30 days for events affecting 500 or more consumers (§314.4(j)).

## 9. Annual report (§314.4(i))

The Qualified Individual reports in writing at least annually to the owner or governing body covering: overall compliance with this program; the risk assessment and control decisions; vendor arrangements; test results; security events and responses; and recommended changes. Keep reports with the operator appendix.
