# WriteOff go-to-market plan — September 25, 2026

Draft for owner review. On September 15 the owner set the order of work: fix the core product and onboarding first, then start go-to-market work ([pre-launch readiness][prelaunch]). The hardening release went live on September 24, so this plan picks up step 5 of that document ("run a focused GTM pilot, then scale what works") and makes it concrete. Nothing here has been executed: no outreach, emails, ad spend or publishing.

Product statements below were checked against `codex/2027-tax-coverage`, the branch `writeoffapp.com` serves. Linked documents live on that branch unless noted.

## 1. Recommendation in brief

1. **Treat October to December as a learning phase and January 4 to April 15, 2027 as the launch.** Freelancers look for tax tools and pay for them mostly in tax season. Use the next ten weeks to prove that a small cohort activates and keeps using the product, so the tax-season push goes into a funnel that works.
2. **Start with one segment: solo knowledge-work freelancers** (designers, developers, writers, marketers, consultants) paid on 1099s, filing as a sole proprietor or single-member LLC, who use a preparer or file themselves. This group falls inside the tax scope the product supports today, and their spending (software, equipment, home office, travel) is where bank import plus AI suggestions saves the most work.
3. **Lead with the preparer package.** "Send your accountant one clean package" is true, easy to demonstrate, and different from how competitors sell. It also makes CPAs and enrolled agents (EAs) with freelance clients a natural referral channel.
4. **Close four gaps before inviting anyone:** the remaining launch gates, the counsel decision on AI-disclosure consent, funnel measurement (the live site probably has no analytics), and the out-of-date default branch.
5. **Decide the tax-season paywall before January.** Today a new user can import all of 2026, review it and download the preparer package inside the free, no-card, 30-day trial, then leave without paying.
6. **No paid ads until the pilot passes its gates.** When ads start, measure them first-party: sending signup events to ad platforms likely counts as disclosing tax return information under IRC §7216.

## 4. Target customer

### 4.1 Beachhead

Solo knowledge-work freelancers and consultants: designers, developers, writers, marketers, virtual assistants and coaches. They are paid on Forms 1099-NEC or 1099-K or by direct deposit, file Schedule C as a sole proprietor or single-member LLC, have no employees or inventory, and either pay a preparer or file with consumer software. Rough revenue range: $30,000 to $200,000.

Why this segment first:

- **Supported scope.** The verified federal coverage is selected 2025/2026 sole proprietor and single-member LLC cases for owner-only businesses. This segment rarely falls outside it.
- **Where the product does the most work.** Recurring software subscriptions, equipment, home office costs and travel appear in bank data and are what the AI suggestions handle best. The profession list in profile setup is already mostly this group.
- **The preparer package fits their year end.** Their preparer asks for organized expenses every spring; the package answers that request directly.
- **Reachable without ads.** They gather on LinkedIn, in freelance marketplace seller communities (Fiverr, Upwork, Contra), in design and developer communities, in freelancer newsletters and at coworking spaces.
- **Willing to pay.** Many already pay for software and a preparer; $149.99 a year is small next to either.

### 4.2 Not yet

| Segment | Why not now | Revisit when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rideshare and delivery drivers | Mileage is their main deduction, and automatic trip detection exists only in the iOS scaffold, which has not been built for a device. Price-sensitive, with free mileage apps as alternatives. | iOS app reaches TestFlight (roadmap Tier 2) |
| Real estate agents | Same dependence on mileage. | iOS app |
| Content creators | Strong content fit, but mixed-use purchases and income from several platforms mean more review questions and support per user. | Test with content only during the pilot |
| W-2 employees with a small side business | Large audience with few business expenses, so less reason to pay. | SEO only |
| S-corporations, businesses with employees, inventory sellers | Outside the supported scope: estimates cover owner-only sole proprietors and single-member LLCs, and exports leave inventory and cost of goods sold to the preparer. | Scope expands |

### 4.3 Validate before committing

Run 15 discovery interviews by November 20 (guide in appendix B), including people who never signed up. Keep the beachhead if most interviewees describe rebuilding their expenses at year end as painful and already spend money or preparer time on it. Switch to the next-best segment if interviews show their preparer or bookkeeping software already solves it, or if pilot users in this segment activate no better than others.

## 5. Positioning and messaging

### 5.1 Positioning (internal)

For solo freelancers and consultants who dread rebuilding a year of expenses for their tax preparer, WriteOff is the year-round expense and tax records app that turns bank transactions and receipts into reviewed, preparer-ready records. AI suggests each category and shows why; only what the user confirms counts toward totals. Unlike Keeper or FlyFin, WriteOff works with whatever preparer or filing software the user already has and makes no promise to find every deduction. Unlike QuickBooks, it is organized around taxes, not bookkeeping.

This extends the roadmap's positioning ("the honest, review-first tax records platform for freelancers") with a specific buyer and a specific year-end outcome. The competitor comparison is for internal use; public comparison pages need dated, sourced statements.

### 5.2 Message pillars

| Message | Proof in the shipped product |
| --- | --- |
| Send your accountant one clean package. | Preparer package: records, transaction CSV, receipts, open questions, README and checksums in one ZIP, with optional 1–7 day share links. |
| AI suggests. You decide. | Each suggestion carries an explanation; totals include only confirmed items; the AI asks before reversing the user's own earlier decisions. |
| Know where you stand before April. | Quarterly planner with a safe-harbor comparison and federal estimates for supported situations; missing facts prompt a question instead of a guess. |
| Billing you can trust. | The trial needs no card and ends on its own; cancel in Settings; saved records stay available on Free. Surprise renewals are a recurring complaint in competitor reviews (roadmap §2). |

### 5.3 Headlines to test

- "Stop rebuilding your year every April."
- "Send your accountant one clean package."
- "Your freelance expenses, reviewed and ready for tax season."

Suggested subhead: "WriteOff imports your transactions, suggests categories with the reasoning shown, and turns what you confirm into records your preparer can use. 30 days free, no card needed."

Segment landing pages (`/for/designers`, `/for/developers`, `/for/consultants`, `/for/writers`) should reuse the pillars with segment-specific expense examples.

### 5.4 Claims and proof rules

- Use the roadmap's claims policy for all copy, including blog posts, emails, partner material and ads. Allowed: "tracks", "suggests", "flags for your review", "records your preparer can use", "planning estimate", "supported situations". Not allowed until built and validated: "finds every deduction", "maximize", guaranteed savings or refunds, "file your taxes", "e-file", audit protection or defense, and "free" for anything that is not free to everyone.
- Testimonials only from real users, with written permission, in their words. If a pilot user received free Premium, say so next to the quote (FTC Endorsement Guides, 16 CFR Part 255).
- No savings figures ("saved $2,200") without a documented, representative basis.
- Keep a dated copy of each published claim alongside the product behavior it relies on.

## 6. Pricing and packaging

In the roadmap's competitor scan, the current price points sit below Keeper ($199 a year and up) and inside FlyFin's range ($84–$348 a year). Both of those include filing and WriteOff does not, so the price holds only if the year-end records are clearly better; the pilot should test that directly (appendix B, question 7). The open questions below are where the paywall sits in tax season and what non-paying users cost.

### 6.1 Decision: the tax-season paywall

A user who signs up in February 2027 can connect a bank, import all of 2026 (trial accounts get extended history), review it and download the preparer package, all within the no-card trial, and never pay. For a product whose biggest demand is a once-a-year job, that is the main revenue leak.

| Option | Effect | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A. Keep as is | Most goodwill and word of mouth. | Seasonal users pay nothing; their model and Plaid costs still apply. |
| B. Gate the preparer package and filing-season exports behind a paid plan; the trial shows a preview of what the package contains | Charges at the moment the user gets the value, after they have done the review work. | Requires changing the Terms ("full feature set"), the FAQ and the homepage line "30 days of Premium reports included". |
| C. Limit trial bank history to 90 days, like Free | Rebuilding a full prior year requires paying. | Users hit the paywall before seeing their own full year, which lowers activation. |

**Recommendation: B, starting January 4, 2027.** Show the price before the user starts the review, and keep saved records available on Free, which avoids the upgrade and downgrade problems in the FTC's H&R Block order. A one-month purchase then works as an informal $14.99 tax-season pass, which is acceptable. Consider a formal one-time tax-year pass only if many users buy one month and cancel.

### 6.2 Annual first in tax season

From January to April, show the annual plan first ("$149.99/year, about $12.50/month") with monthly as the alternative. Annual captures the season's intent and covers the months users would otherwise cancel. It is also cheaper to collect: one card charge a year instead of twelve.

### 6.3 Decision: bank sync for Free and expired accounts

The scheduled sync (`functions/src/index.ts`) selects every active Plaid connection, regardless of plan. Plaid bills Transactions per connected Item per month (confirm against the contract), so each Free or expired user with a linked bank costs money every month with no revenue. Options: keep syncing (simplest, most goodwill); pause automatic sync after the trial, with a clear notice and a "sync now" button; or remove the Item after a notice period while keeping saved records. Recommendation: pause automatic sync after the trial, and state it on the pricing page.

### 6.4 Unit costs to watch in the pilot

- **Model calls.** The [scale review][scale] estimated $0.0005–$0.0015 per transaction analysis, about $0.06–$0.18 per active user per month at 120 transactions. A trial user importing two years of history (median about 3,000 transactions in that model) triggers roughly $1.50–$4.50 of model calls on day one. The estimate assumed `gpt-4o-mini`; production now uses GPT-4.1-mini for transactions, which has higher list prices, so treat these figures as a floor and measure actual spend per signup.
- **Plaid.** Per-Item monthly fees, including non-paying users (section 6.3).
- **Payments.** At Stripe's standard card rate (2.9% + 30¢), about $4.65 per annual sale and $0.73 per monthly charge.

Margins on paying users look healthy. The cost risk is non-paying users with bank connections, and it grows fastest exactly when the tax-season push works.

## 7. Channels

Two constraints shape the mix. First, the budget is undecided, and the pilot should not need one. Second, conversion tracking through ad platforms conflicts with §7216. Treasury regulations treat registration data for tax software as tax return information (§301.7216-1(b)(3), Example 1, as summarized in the [§7216 review][s7216]), so sending a "signed up" event with an identifier to Meta or Google Ads likely needs a separate consent that cannot be required for service. Get counsel's answer before installing any ad pixel or conversion API, and never install one on `/auth`, `/login`, onboarding or signed-in pages. That favors owned, earned and partner channels.

## 8. Funnel, measurement and gates

### 8.1 Funnel stages

| Stage | Definition | Where it is recorded today |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Signed up | Firebase Auth account created | Firebase Auth |
| Set up | Profile saved with current consents | `user_profiles.consents_recorded_at` |
| Data in | Active bank connection, or first manual or receipt expense | `plaid_connections`, transactions |
| Activated | First expense reviewed and confirmed (the definition in [pre-launch readiness][prelaunch]) | Transaction `review_status` |
| Retained in week 4 | Confirmed at least one expense 22–28 days after signup | Review timestamps |
| Paid | Verified Stripe plan | `user_profiles.subscriptionPlan` |
| Package | Preparer package built | `preparer_handoffs` |

A weekly, read-only admin script can compute these as aggregate counts per signup week from data that already exists, like the existing production inventory scripts. It needs no third-party tool. Aggregate internal counts are the lowest-risk option under §7216; include them in the counsel review anyway. Add `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign` and the referring domain to the profile at signup, plus the optional "How did you hear about us?" question. With cohorts this small, the self-reported answer is the most reliable attribution.

### 8.2 Gates to start the tax-season push (proposed)

These are starting thresholds to revisit after the first full 30-day cohort. With 25–40 pilot users, treat them as directional.

- At least 60% of new signups finish setup.
- At least 40% activate within 7 days of signup.
- At least 25% of activated users are still confirming expenses in week 4.
- At least 80% of Plaid Link attempts end in a connected account.
- At least 40% of activated beachhead users say they would be "very disappointed" if they could no longer use WriteOff (the Sean Ellis question).
- No data-integrity incident, and support fits in about five hours a week.
- A trial-to-paid baseline measured on users who did not get free Premium.

If a gate fails, fix the step it measures before adding traffic.

## Appendix A: pilot invitation

> Hi {name}, I'm Pratham. I'm building WriteOff, an app that organizes freelance expenses into records you can hand to your accountant. I'm looking for 20 freelancers to use it through this tax season and tell me what's missing. You'd get Premium free through April 15, 2027. ## Appendix B: interview guide

Ask about what people did, not what they would do.

1. Walk me through how you handled expenses for your 2025 taxes. When did you start?
2. Who prepared your return? What did they ask you for, and how long did it take to pull together?
3. What do you use today: a spreadsheet, QuickBooks, Keeper, bank exports, nothing? Why that?
4. What was the most annoying part? What did it cost you in time, fees or missed items?
5. How do you handle quarterly estimated payments?
6. Have you paid for anything to help with this? How much, and was it worth it?
7. (Show the sample preparer package.) What would your preparer think of this? What's missing?
8. If this worked, what would you stop doing?

Strong signals: they already spend money or preparer time on this, and they describe the year-end rebuild as painful without being prompted.
