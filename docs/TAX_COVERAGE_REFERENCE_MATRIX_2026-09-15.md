# Federal tax coverage and release references

Reviewed September 17, 2026. This describes the staging implementation. See [staging validation](STAGING_VALIDATION_2026-09-15.md) for deployment and live checks; this is not complete-return certification. Earlier broad platform counts do not validate tax eligibility or every taxpayer situation.

## Intended initial scope

Recordkeeping, receipt review and accountant handoff can serve a wider audience than the calculator. Federal planning estimates should initially be limited to screened simple cases: ordinary wages and/or one domestic Schedule C business (a positive profit, or a loss with explicit at-risk, material-participation and profit-motive declarations), confirmed expenses, reconciled income, explicit year-specific personal deduction facts, and below-threshold QBI without carryovers or special deductions/credits. The new personal-deduction path handles supported age/blindness, dependent-standard-deduction and married-separate spouse-itemization facts. Eligible 2025–2026 seniors receive the separate enhanced deduction, including its MAGI phaseout. Reviewed ordinary Social Security cases use the published worksheet. Recorded short-term/long-term capital gain character, the current-year capital loss limit, and the Schedule 1-A qualified tips, W-2 overtime and passenger-vehicle-loan-interest deductions (plus the 2026 non-itemizer charitable deduction) are calculated from explicit organizer facts; unanswered intake yields no deduction and is never treated as No. The current app does **not** enforce this entire screen; the exclusions below remain release gates for broader tax claims.

Published annual parameter sets exist for 2024, 2025 and 2026. The calculator rejects 2027. Do not represent all 2027 annual amounts or final filing forms as known. Bracket calculations are planning amounts; IRS tax-table rounding and complete returns are not certified.

## Income reconciliation implemented in this batch

- Posted transactions in the selected year count when explicitly categorized `income`/`revenue`, or entered as income in the manual account. A negative bank amount alone is insufficient: the database normalizer derives `type: income` from the amount sign, including transfers and refunds.
- Transaction identifiers deduplicate repeated reads. Unclassified bank credits are excluded with a calculation warning; they are not silently classified as taxable business receipts.
- Gross receipts, income transactions and 1099 forms are alternative evidence that can overlap. Without a verified link, mixed sources return HTTP 422 `INCOME_RECONCILIATION_REQUIRED`; no guessed total is produced. Multiple unlinked 1099 forms also require review, since a NEC and K can report overlapping earnings.
- New platform-summary imports store the generated receipt's ID on the associated 1099. The tax snapshot counts the receipt once when its linked document has the same amount and import provenance. Missing links or gross/net mismatches require review. Historical imports are not automatically matched by name or amount.
- Standalone business NEC/K amounts can be used. MISC, INT, DIV and B forms, and gross receipts explicitly entered as rental/investment income, require classification review rather than automatic Schedule C treatment.
- Profile income projections are not added as another income source. Detailed W-2 documents take precedence over legacy profile withholding. Detailed retirement contributions take precedence over the corresponding aggregate profile amount.
- The JSON 1040 estimate and Form1040 PDF now call the same federal snapshot. Schedule C export and Schedule SE auto use the same business-income reconciliation policy. Quarterly summaries and reminders now consume the annual federal response; they withhold payment recommendations pending separate full-year/payment review.
- The four supported filing-status labels saved by onboarding are normalized to the engine keys before federal calculations; the PDF selects the same status. Unsupported or invalid nonempty labels return actionable HTTP 422 `FILING_STATUS_REVIEW_REQUIRED`. Qualifying widow(er)/surviving spouse is explicitly review-blocked, not treated as Single. Legacy profiles without a status retain the existing Single default and require profile review; this is not an eligibility determination.
- The dashboard tax cards use the shared federal endpoint and its review/error states; they no longer derive Schedule C profit or quarterly tax from sign-based cash flow. Other profile-based savings helpers use filing-status normalization; savings, monthly-deduction and profit/loss endpoints return the same 422 for invalid statuses. Existing simplified formulas and 2025 default parameters in those other helpers are unchanged and remain planning limitations. Call sites without any profile still use their existing generic rate assumption.

- Owner-recorded reconciliation decisions (`income_reconciliations/{id}`, server-only, owner-scoped, exported and deleted with the account) resolve the gate without editing records. A decision names specific records by reference with their amounts at decision time and one of three treatments: *same payments* (a 1099 and the receipts and/or bank deposits it reports count once; a K and a NEC may also be declared the same stream), *separate income* (one record counts in addition to everything else), or *1099-K includes fees* (gross receipts use the 1099-K gross; the fee becomes an expense candidate flagged for review, never an automatic deduction). Amounts must agree before a decision applies; nothing is matched by payer or amount without the owner's confirmation.
- The engine re-validates every saved decision against the current records on each calculation. A decision whose records changed, disappeared or were claimed twice is reported as a stale conflict and the 422 remains. Unreconciled records beside a reconciled stream, and any 1099 without its own decision when a year has several, still return the 422. The 422 body lists the conflicting records by kind, id, label and amount only, with a deep link to Income → Reconcile (`/api/income/reconciliation` is the same explainer as JSON).

The 422 is an explicit review requirement, not a claim that records have been reconciled. Do not advise deleting supporting records merely to bypass this gate; the Reconcile tab records how they relate instead.

The IRS requires reporting taxable business income even without an information return. Information returns must be reconciled with underlying records, not automatically added to the receipts they describe. Sources: [IRS gig economy tax center](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/gig-economy-tax-center), [understanding Form 1099-K](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/understanding-your-form-1099-k).

It does not prepare a state return. ## Remaining unsupported scenarios and release gaps

1. **Unlinked overlapping income:** the Reconcile tab records owner decisions but there is still no historical payment provenance; proposed candidates are only the owner's own records, never suggested matches. Linked imports do not yet provide file-level idempotency across repeated uploads. Bank deposits can be net of fees; a matching deposit total does not prove gross receipts, and a recorded platform fee is an expense candidate the owner must still confirm.
2. **Social Security, investments and retirement:** supported ordinary SSA/RRB cases now collect and calculate the published worksheet facts, with JSON/PDF/quarterly parity. Incomplete, foreign, prior-year lump-sum, special IRA and unreconciled adjustment cases remain review-blocked. Capital gains/losses, business losses and rental amounts still require additional classification before this benefit worksheet path (that gate is unchanged). Outside the benefit path, the organizer now records net short-term and net long-term amounts separately: short-term gains are ordinary income, long-term gains use the 0/15/20% stacking, and net losses are limited to $3,000 ($1,500 married filing separately) with the carryforward reported as a warning. A nonzero legacy combined total returns `CAPITAL_GAIN_REVIEW_REQUIRED` instead of being assumed long-term. Collectibles (28%) and unrecaptured §1250 gain, prior-year capital loss carryovers, wash sales, basis records, qualified-dividend character, retirement basis/penalties and rental/passive-activity treatment remain unmodeled. [Benefit review validation](TAX_SOCIAL_SECURITY_VALIDATION_2026-09-16.md), [Publication915](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p915), [Topic409](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc409).
3. **Dependents and credits:** a generic dependent count now triggers `DEPENDENT_CREDIT_REVIEW_REQUIRED` before an annual estimate or PDF. It does not establish child age, residency, support, SSN or EITC eligibility. All positive potential EITC now requires review because the organizer does not collect complete valid-SSN, U.S.-home and qualifying-child-of-another facts; claimable taxpayer/spouse cases receive a specific dependency explanation. No-child EITC age tests use saved DOB, with the specific IRS January1 boundaries. Exact EITC tables and all ACTC alternatives are not covered. [Child Tax Credit requirements](https://www.irs.gov/credits-deductions/individuals/child-tax-credit).
4. **Filing status and household facts:** reviewed age/blindness/dependent standard deductions and married-separate spouse itemization are implemented. Missing/year-stale facts return `PERSONAL_DEDUCTION_REVIEW_REQUIRED`. Community property, nonresident/short-year/deceased special returns and separate spouse SE wage bases remain outside the supported path. Married W-2 amounts must not be assumed to belong to the self-employed spouse.
5. **Losses and complex QBI:** a Schedule C loss is no longer clamped to $0. It offsets other income only after the organizer records that all investment is at risk (Schedule C line 32a), the taxpayer materially participated (line G) and the activity is carried on for profit (§183); otherwise, or when any answer is No, the estimate returns `BUSINESS_LOSS_REVIEW_REQUIRED`. The §461(l) excess business loss limit ($256,000/$512,000 joint for 2026; $313,000/$626,000 for 2025; $305,000/$610,000 for 2024) is applied to this one business, SE tax is $0 in a loss year, and the QBI deduction is $0 with a qualified-business-loss carryforward warning. Not modeled: Forms 6198 and 8582 limitation amounts, aggregation of several businesses for §461(l), net operating loss computation and carryforward (AGI is floored at $0 with a warning), QBI loss carryovers, high-income wage/property/SSTB limitations and the 2026 minimum active-business deduction. [Schedule C instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1040sc), [Form461 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i461), [Form8995 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8995), [Publication536](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p536).
6. **Deduction eligibility:** health-insurance, HSA, retirement and student-loan inputs do not establish every limit, phaseout, coverage test or allowable deduction. The enhanced senior deduction is implemented for eligible 2025–2026 cases. The Schedule 1-A qualified tips (self-employed: min of tips, $25,000 and Schedule C net profit), W-2 qualified overtime ($12,500/$25,000) and personal-use passenger vehicle loan interest ($10,000) deductions, with their statutory MAGI phaseouts and married-filing-separately exclusions, and the 2026 §170(p) non-itemizer charitable deduction ($1,000/$2,000) are calculated from explicit organizer declarations and applied below AGI before the QBI cap. Review-blocked rather than estimated: tips in an SSTB (Notice 2025-69 transition relief), business-use vehicle interest, overtime paid to contractors, and MAGI foreign/territory add-backs. Not modeled: whether SE health insurance, half-SE-tax and retirement deductions also reduce the tips business-income limit (left open by T.D. 10044), Schedule 1-A amounts from multiple businesses or a spouse's W-2s, the 2026 itemized 0.5%-of-AGI charitable floor, alternative minimum tax and net investment income tax. See [personal deduction scope](TAX_PERSONAL_DEDUCTIONS_2026-09-16.md).
7. **Home office:** the simplified method (Rev. Proc. 2013-13) is implemented from saved Settings facts — regular and exclusive use, qualifying use (§280A(c)(1)), rented/owned, square footage capped at 300, months of use — as $5 × allowable square feet × months/12, limited to Schedule C line 29 tentative profit with no carryover, and flows into Schedule C line 30 before Schedule SE, QBI and Form1040 through the shared snapshot. Eligibility is the taxpayer's declaration, not a determination: unanswered facts return `HOME_OFFICE_REVIEW_REQUIRED`; a "no" answer yields $0. The daycare and inventory-storage exceptions, multiple homes and any actual-expense (Form8829) case remain review-blocked because Settings do not collect the line 8 income ordering, mortgage-interest/real-estate-tax allocation, owned-home depreciation, casualty losses or prior carryovers; only the separately supplied rented-home operating-expense context in `calc8829()` is calculated. The `/protected/form8829` page and `form8829` export produce a labeled planning worksheet, not Form8829. [Form8829 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8829), [Publication587](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p587).
8. **Assets:** first-year nonlisted 5/7-year MACRS half-year calculations, plus (a) the de minimis safe harbor for items at or under $2,500 in a year the taxpayer records the Reg. §1.263(a)-1(f) election in Settings — treated as a current Schedule C expense, never depreciation, with the asset record treated as one substantiated item (invoice grouping is not verified) — and (b) Section179 for first-year, nonlisted 5/7-year property with more than 50% business use placed in service in 2025 or 2026, limited to the published year dollar limit and phaseout and to business taxable income under §179(b)(3) (Schedule C profit before §179 plus W-2 wages), with the carryover reported and basis reduced immediately. The election year is recorded on the calculation. Bonus depreciation, vehicles and listed property (§280F), ≤50% business use, prior-year history, straight-line, mid-quarter and 2024/2027 §179 elections return review-required responses. The §179/home-office ordering is a planning simplification: when the §179 income limit binds, line 29 is already at or below zero, so no home office amount is produced. [Publication946](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p946), [Form4562 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i4562).
9. **Quarterly planning:** authenticated summaries now share the annual calculation and withhold installment/penalty verdicts. The public tool illustrates original regular-method installments using reviewed annual tax, withholding, prior-year AGI and explicit eligibility facts; missing exemption facts require review. It does not calculate an annual return, current payment balance, annualized income or Form2210 penalties. Unsupported generated vouchers are blocked. Payment timing, changed filing status, special rules and relief still need separate review. [Quarterly validation](TAX_QUARTERLY_VALIDATION_2026-09-16.md), [2026 Publication505](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p505).
10. **Other taxes/payments:** SSA/RRB withholding is integrated once into annual payments and quarterly summaries. Other 1099 withholding and all Additional Medicare withholding reconciliation are not fully integrated. State amounts are informational planning estimates from the year-labeled registry above: California and Ohio are 2025-only, every other state outside the ten encoded ones and every 2027 request is unsupported, state itemized deductions/credits are not modeled, and the estimate is never added to the federal total. Local income taxes (NYC, Yonkers, Ohio municipal/school district, PA earned income tax, Metro SHS) and separate business taxes are surfaced as notices only, never calculated. Disaster relief is not covered.
11. **Presentation and filing:** matching PDF/JSON totals is not proof of an official, complete or e-file-ready IRS return. Provider/OCR accuracy and real staging journeys require separate validation.
