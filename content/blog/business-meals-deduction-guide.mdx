Business meals are one of the most commonly claimed deductions and one of the most commonly claimed incorrectly. The IRS pays close attention to this category, "we talked about work" does not make a dinner deductible, and the 100% restaurant meal deduction from 2021-2022 is gone and not coming back.

Get this right and you are deducting real money. Get it wrong and you are inviting a follow-up letter.

## The Rule: 50% Deductible

All qualifying business meals are deductible at **50%**. That covers food, drinks, tax, and tip. The half-off reality applies to all of it.

## Three Requirements Every Meal Needs

Under IRS Publication 463, a meal must have all three:

**1. Genuine business purpose.** Not "we were both there and discussed a project." There needs to be a substantive reason the meeting was called: reviewing a contract, discussing a proposal, closing a deal.

**2. A real business relationship.** The other person must be a client, prospect, employee, contractor, or professional advisor connected to your work.

**3. Not lavish or extravagant.** Reasonable costs for the situation.

You do not need to talk about business the entire time. But the meeting needs to have been scheduled for an actual business reason, not because you were both hungry.

## What the IRS Requires You to Document

For every business meal, record the amount (keep the receipt), the date, the restaurant name and location, the business purpose, and the names and business relationships of everyone present.

A credit card statement showing $87 at a restaurant is not documentation. That same receipt with a note reading "Dinner with Sarah Chen, Apex Marketing prospect, discussed Q3 campaign scope" is.

Write the note at the time. Reconstructed notes from months later get treated very differently by auditors.

**Practical systems that work:** photo the receipt and add a note in your phone immediately after, use an expense app with built-in receipt capture and purpose fields, or keep detailed calendar entries paired with receipts.

## How Different Scenarios Work

**Client meals:** 50% deductible. Document the client, their company, and what was discussed.

**Solo meals while traveling:** All meals qualify at 50% when you are away from home overnight on a genuine business trip, even eating alone. The business trip is what makes the meals business-related.

**Office snacks for client meetings:** 50% deductible as business meals.

**Company holiday parties and all-hands events:** 100% deductible as goodwill expenses, not classified as business meals.

## Entertainment Is Completely Dead

Pre-2018, you could deduct 50% of entertainment: sports tickets, concerts, theater. The Tax Cuts and Jobs Act killed that entirely. Entertainment deductions are now zero, regardless of business purpose.

Taking a client to a baseball game? The tickets are $0 deductible. But a dinner at a restaurant before the game where you actually discuss business is 50% deductible as a separate expense. Keep those receipts separate from the tickets.

## Common Mistakes That Get Meals Disallowed

Calling regular grocery runs "business meals." Claiming frequent dinners with a spouse or friends who happen to also be business contacts. No receipt, no notes, no documentation. Treating a social event as a business meeting because the topic came up briefly.

The IRS targets personal dining that has been relabeled as business, not genuine client development with proper records. Document it properly and this is a completely defensible deduction.
