Every year, millions of people sit down to file their taxes and realize they are missing something. A 1099 from a forgotten client. Their quarterly payment confirmation numbers. A health insurance statement buried in an old email.

Use this checklist to gather common records before you file or meet your tax preparer. Your circumstances may require additional documents.

Start with one month using the free [freelance expense reset checklist](/resources/freelance-expense-reset). Check off six organizing steps, then print your checklist for the next review.

## Personal Information

- Social Security Number (or ITIN) for you, your spouse, and any dependents
- Date of birth for all filers and dependents
- Prior year AGI (Form 1040, Line 11, required for e-file identity verification)
- IP PIN if you have one; see the IRS link below for eligibility and how to obtain one
- Bank account and routing number for direct deposit or direct debit

## Income Documents

**Business income:**
- All 1099-NEC forms received; include business income even when no form was issued
- All 1099-K forms received (from Stripe, PayPal, Venmo Business, Etsy, Uber, DoorDash, Amazon, eBay, or any payment platform)
- Platform annual tax summaries: reconcile gross payments, fees, refunds and transfers with the forms and your records to avoid omissions or double counting
- Records of cash income, Venmo/Zelle received outside a business account, or any income without a form

**W-2 income (if applicable):**
- W-2 from every employer you worked for during the year
- If you received both a W-2 and a 1099-NEC from the same company (rare but possible), have both

**Other income:**
- 1099-INT for bank interest (from every bank account, even small amounts)
- 1099-DIV for dividends
- 1099-B for stock sales (your brokerage's annual tax statement)
- 1099-R if you took a retirement account distribution
- SSA-1099 if you received Social Security benefits
- Unemployment compensation documents (if applicable)
- Alimony received (for pre-2019 divorce agreements only)

## Business Expense Records

Keep evidence of the payee, amount, payment, date, purchase or service, and business purpose for expenses you claim. A combination of receipts, invoices, statements and other records may be needed; a payment record alone may not establish deductibility. Travel, gifts and vehicle costs have additional substantiation rules. See the [IRS supporting-records guidance](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/what-kind-of-records-should-i-keep).

**Organized by Schedule C line:**
- Advertising: receipts for ads, social media promotions, marketing materials
- Car and truck: mileage log with date, destination, business purpose, miles; odometer readings January 1 and December 31; total business miles for the year
- Home office: square footage of dedicated workspace; total home square footage; annual costs for mortgage interest/rent, utilities, insurance, and repairs
- Insurance: health insurance premium statements if deducting separately; business liability insurance
- Legal and professional: CPA fees, attorney fees, consultant invoices
- Office expense: receipts for computer equipment, supplies, software
- Rent: lease agreements or receipts for office or equipment rental
- Travel: receipts for flights, hotels, transportation; business purpose documentation
- Meals: receipts plus a record of who was present and the business discussion
- Utilities: phone and internet bills if claiming business use percentage
- Other expenses: any recurring business cost that does not fit above categories

## Deductions That Have Their Own Forms

**Home office (Form 8829):**
- Office square footage and total home square footage
- Annual home expenses: mortgage interest (from Form 1098), real estate taxes, rent payments, homeowner's insurance, utilities, repairs attributable to the home

**Vehicle depreciation (Form 4562):**
- Date the vehicle was placed in service for business
- Total miles driven and business miles driven
- Original purchase price if using actual expense method

**Depreciation on equipment:**
- Date of purchase and original cost for any major equipment purchased in the year
- Prior year depreciation schedules if you are carrying forward

**Retirement contributions:**
- SEP-IRA: contribution amount and the financial institution name
- Solo 401(k): employee deferral amount and employer contribution amount separately

## Payments and Credits

**Estimated tax payments:**
- Amount and date of each quarterly payment (IRS Direct Pay confirmation numbers)
- Any state estimated tax payments made during the year

**Other credits:**
- Form 1095-A if you have a Marketplace health plan (needed to reconcile premium tax credits)
- Childcare provider name, address, and EIN/SSN if claiming Child and Dependent Care Credit
- Tuition paid records if claiming education credits
- Qualified retirement contribution records for Saver's Credit

## Prior Year Carryforwards

- Net operating loss carryforward from prior years
- Capital loss carryforward (from your prior year Schedule D)
- Charitable contribution carryforward if you exceeded the AGI limit
- AMT credit if applicable

## If You Are Filing With a Professional

In addition to everything above, your tax preparer will need:
- A copy of last year's return
- Authorization to access IRS records or represent you if needed; your preparer can explain whether Form 2848 or 8821 applies
- If they are e-filing on your behalf: Form 8879 signed and dated (this is the authorization)

Organized records can reduce follow-up questions. Ask your preparer which documents they need and how they price the work.

## A Note on Document Retention

Retention depends on the return and the record. The IRS generally specifies three years after filing, with longer periods for some refund claims, omitted income, worthless securities and other circumstances; early returns are treated as filed on the due date. Property records generally need to remain available through the limitations period for the year of disposition. Keep filed returns and check the [IRS retention guidance](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/how-long-should-i-keep-records) before disposing of supporting records.
