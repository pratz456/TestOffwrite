If you take contract or freelance development work as a sole proprietor or single-member LLC, your business income and expenses go on **Schedule C**. Each legitimate expense reduces both income tax and the 15.3% self-employment tax.

Every deduction has to be **ordinary and necessary** for your development business (IRC §162). Mixed-use costs, like a laptop you also use personally, need a reasonable business-use percentage.

## Cloud, hosting and API costs

Infrastructure you pay for to build, test or run client work is deductible:

- Cloud and hosting (AWS, Google Cloud, Azure, Vercel, Netlify, DigitalOcean)
- Domains, DNS, email and SSL services
- CI minutes, monitoring, logging and error-tracking services
- Usage-based APIs, including AI model APIs

If you bill a client for these costs, the amount you bill is income and the cost is your expense. Keep the invoices so the two match up.

## Developer tools and subscriptions

- Code hosting and editors (GitHub, GitLab, JetBrains)
- AI coding assistants and design tools
- Password managers, test-device services and staging tools
- Project, time-tracking and invoicing software

Subscriptions used only for business are deductible in the year you pay for them. For shared plans, deduct the business share.

## Hardware and test devices

Laptops, monitors, keyboards, docking stations and phones or tablets bought for testing are business assets. You can usually expense items up to **$2,500 per item or invoice** under the de minimis safe harbor election, use **Section 179** (up to $2,560,000 for 2026, limited to your business income), or take **bonus depreciation**, generally 100% for qualifying property acquired and placed in service after January 19, 2025. Record the purchase date, cost and business-use percentage. The [Section 179 guide](/blog/section-179-equipment-deduction-guide) explains the trade-offs.

## Home office and internet

A dedicated area of your home used **regularly and exclusively** as your principal place of business qualifies for the home office deduction: $5 per square foot up to 300 square feet with the simplified method, or actual costs on Form 8829. Deduct the business share of internet and phone service. See the [home office guide](/blog/home-office-deduction-guide) for the requirements.

## Platform and payment fees

Service fees charged by Upwork, Toptal, Contra or similar platforms, and payment-processing fees from Stripe or PayPal, are deductible. A Form 1099-K usually shows **gross** payments before fees, so report the gross amount as income and deduct the fees separately.

## Contractors and subcontractors

Paying another developer, designer or QA tester is a business expense. For a U.S. contractor, collect a **Form W-9**; for payments made in 2026 you generally file **Form 1099-NEC** if you pay them **$2,000 or more** (it was $600 for 2025 payments). For a foreign contractor who does the work outside the United States, collect **Form W-8BEN**; a 1099-NEC generally isn't required.

## Training, books and conferences

Courses, certifications, books and conference tickets that **maintain or improve skills** you use in your current work are deductible (Treas. Reg. §1.162-5), along with travel to attend. A bootcamp that qualifies you for a different trade isn't.

## Insurance

Professional liability (errors and omissions), general liability and cyber insurance for your business are deductible business expenses.

## Income questions developers often get wrong

- **Foreign clients.** Payments from clients abroad are still taxable income even though no 1099 arrives. Report them in U.S. dollars.
- **Missing 1099s.** Clients who paid you less than $2,000 in 2026 may not send a 1099-NEC. The income is still taxable.
- **Payment apps.** The federal Form 1099-K threshold is more than $20,000 and more than 200 transactions, but some states use lower thresholds, and all of the income is taxable either way.
- **Crypto payments.** Crypto received for services is income at its fair market value when you receive it. See our [crypto guide](/blog/crypto-taxes-freelancers-guide).

## Deductions outside Schedule C

- **Self-employment tax:** deduct half on Schedule 1.
- **Health insurance:** the self-employed health insurance deduction, if you aren't eligible for a subsidized plan through an employer, including a spouse's.
- **Retirement:** SEP-IRA or solo 401(k) contributions, with a combined 2026 limit of $72,000.
- **QBI deduction:** up to 20% of qualified business income. Below 2026 taxable income of $201,750 ($403,500 joint), the type of business doesn't matter. Above it, work that is primarily advice can count as consulting, which is a specified service trade with stricter limits, so ask your preparer how your work is classified.

## Quarterly estimated taxes

If you expect to owe at least $1,000, pay estimated tax by April 15, June 15 and September 15, 2026, and January 15, 2027. Paying 100% of last year's tax (110% if your prior-year AGI was over $150,000) generally avoids the underpayment penalty. Try the [quarterly estimate calculator](/tools/quarterly-estimate-calculator) for a starting figure.

## Records to keep

Keep invoices and receipts with the date, amount and business purpose, and note the client when a cost relates to one project. Cloud bills are easier to support when accounts or projects are named by client.
