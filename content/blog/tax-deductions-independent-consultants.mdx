Independent consultants who work as sole proprietors or single-member LLCs report business income and expenses on **Schedule C**. Consulting has two tax features most freelancers don't: a lot of client travel, and a special limit on the 20% qualified business income deduction at higher incomes. Both are worth planning for.

As with any business, a deduction must be **ordinary and necessary** for your consulting practice (IRC §162), and personal costs don't qualify because they help you work.

## Travel to clients

**Local trips.** Commuting to a regular work location is personal. But if your home office is your principal place of business, trips from home to client sites are deductible business travel. Travel between home and a temporary work location, one expected to last a year or less, is also deductible (Rev. Rul. 99-7).

For 2026 the standard mileage rate is **72.5 cents per mile from January through June and 76 cents per mile from July through December**, or you can deduct the business share of actual car costs. Parking and tolls for business trips are deductible either way.

**Overnight trips.** When business takes you away from your tax home overnight, airfare, lodging, rideshares, laundry and **50% of meals** are deductible. On a trip that is primarily for business within the United States, transportation is deductible even if you add personal days, but the costs of the personal days aren't. An assignment expected to last more than a year isn't temporary, so its travel costs generally aren't deductible.

## Home office

If you use part of your home **regularly and exclusively** for your practice, and it's your principal place of business, you can deduct home office costs. Administrative and management work, such as proposals, invoicing and scheduling, counts toward principal place of business when there's no other fixed location where you do substantial administrative work. See our [home office guide](/blog/home-office-deduction-guide).

## Professional costs

- Professional liability (errors and omissions) and general liability insurance
- Licenses, certifications and continuing education that maintain or improve skills you use now
- Legal and accounting fees for the business
- Research subscriptions, data services and industry reports

## Software and tools

CRM, proposal and e-signature software, video conferencing, project management and presentation tools are deductible when used for the business. Deduct only the business share of anything you also use personally.

## Subcontractors

Paying another consultant, researcher or designer is a business expense. Collect a **Form W-9** before you pay. For payments made in 2026, you generally file **Form 1099-NEC** for anyone you pay **$2,000 or more**; the threshold was $600 for 2025 payments.

## Client meals, gifts and marketing

- **Meals** with clients or prospects are generally **50%** deductible when you're present, the meal isn't lavish and there's a business purpose. Note who attended and what you discussed.
- **Business gifts** are deductible up to **$25 per recipient per year**. See [the $25 rule](/blog/business-gifts-25-dollar-limit).
- **Marketing**, including your website, speaking costs and conference booths, is deductible. Entertainment, such as event tickets, isn't.

## The QBI limit that applies to consulting

The qualified business income (QBI) deduction can be worth up to 20% of your business income, but consulting is a **specified service trade or business**. For 2026:

- Below taxable income of **$201,750** ($403,500 on a joint return), you get the full deduction.
- Between $201,750 and **$276,750** ($403,500 and $553,500 joint), the deduction for consulting income phases out.
- Above $276,750 ($553,500 joint), consulting income gets no QBI deduction.

Deductions that lower taxable income, such as retirement contributions, can change where you land in that range, but some of them also reduce QBI itself, so ask your preparer to model it before you rely on it. Consultants in this range often also look at whether an S corporation makes sense; see [sole proprietor vs. LLC vs. S corp](/blog/sole-proprietor-vs-llc-vs-scorp-taxes) and [reasonable salary rules](/blog/reasonable-salary-s-corp-irs).

## Retirement and health insurance

A **solo 401(k)** or **SEP-IRA** lets you shelter a large share of consulting income, with a combined 2026 limit of $72,000; see our [retirement guide](/blog/retirement-accounts-self-employed). The self-employed health insurance deduction applies if you aren't eligible for a subsidized plan through an employer, including a spouse's.

## Quarterly taxes with uneven income

Consulting income often arrives in large, irregular payments. Estimated tax is due April 15, June 15 and September 15, 2026, and January 15, 2027. Two ways to avoid underpayment penalties:

- **Safe harbor:** pay 100% of last year's tax, or **110%** if your prior-year AGI was over $150,000.
- **Annualized income method:** if most of your income arrives late in the year, Form 2210 Schedule AI can match required payments to when you actually earned the income.

The [quarterly estimate calculator](/tools/quarterly-estimate-calculator) gives a starting figure.

## Records to keep

Travel, gifts and car expenses have strict substantiation rules. Record the amount, date, place and business purpose, and for meals, who attended and your business relationship. A mileage log with the date, destination, purpose and miles for each trip is essential if you drive to clients.
